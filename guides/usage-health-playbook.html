Internal guide, implementation

Driving usage health score by product

When a metric shows yellow or red in Totango, start here. Each product lists the quickest ways to move its metrics, in the session and between calls.

Internal only. Do not share with customers.

How to use this guide

Check Totango first to see which usage metrics are red or yellow on your accounts. Each metric is tied to a specific, observable customer behavior in the platform, and the tips are ways to prompt that behavior during calls, workshops, or async follow-up. Work the metrics that are weakest on a given account. You don't need to run every tactic on every customer.

Screen-sharing with the customer driving is the most reliable way to move any of these. Where it applies, it's the first tip.

Explore / Media Intelligence

Three metrics make up usage health here. Searches and Articles Clicked reflect whether the customer is actively working in the platform. % MAU reflects whether the people who should be in the platform actually are.

Searches Completed (90D)

Measures whether the customer is actively interacting with a search: building, editing, or saving one. The goal is hands-on-keyboard engagement with the search engine itself, not just viewing results someone else set up.

  • Get them hands-on, screen-share style. Have the customer share their screen and do one of the following live: build a Monitor View, edit an existing search with new keywords, add a filter to a search and save it, or build a new search from scratch.
  • Use email as a lighter-touch nudge. Ask them to review an existing search and add keywords you may not have known to include, like exclusions, product names, or leadership names. Or simply encourage them to try out Monitor.
Draft email: search review nudge
Subject: Quick favor on your [Brand] search

Hi [Name],

When you have five minutes, could you open your [Search name] search and add anything we might have missed? Exclusions, product names, executive names, competitor terms, anything you'd want to see more or less of.

If it's easier, I'm happy to hop on a 15-minute screen-share and do it together.

Thanks,
[Your name]

Articles Clicked (90D)

Measures whether the customer is reading the content the platform surfaces. Daily Digest is the strongest lever here. If it's compelling enough to open and click through, this metric moves on its own without any extra outreach.

  • Make the Digest worth opening. Coverage of their own brand is table stakes. The real lift comes from content that's tangential but genuinely interesting to them.
  • Add an industry pulse. What's the highest-reaching news in their industry today? A topic-level boolean works well for surfacing this automatically.
  • Go hyper-local if relevant. If the customer cares about a specific city or market, what's the big local story they'd want to know about?
  • Build a "top stories" layer. Think beyond their direct mentions. Set up a top 10-20 reach digest covering the broader conversation they should be aware of.

% MAU (90D)

Measures what share of provisioned users are actually logging in. A low number here is often a data hygiene issue as much as an adoption issue. Stale or auto-created users can drag this down without reflecting real engagement.

  • Drive logins directly. Get more people actually using the platform, or clean up users who were auto-created and never activated.
  • Loop in the champion. Ask your champion to flag any users on the account who haven't logged in yet. They often know why, and can help re-engage them.
  • Validate the user list. Confirm which users are actually still needed and delete any that aren't. A smaller, accurate user base is better for this metric than a large, stale one.

MIRA Studio

One KPI here, and it rewards customers who work through the full Mira Studio workflow, not just ask one question. When it slips, the customer is usually set up and trained but never built a habit, or doesn't see how it fits their week. The goal is to get them back in with something specific to try.

HS20 Mira 90D

Because almost every click counts, a customer who goes beyond one question (editing the canvas, sharing, generating a report, giving feedback) moves this metric more than one who asks and leaves.

Definition

Average # of Mira Studio usage events per active user in the last 90 days. While most Mira Studio usage is asking questions, this metric includes almost every click in the product (e.g. opening the side panel to see Mira history, editing the resulting canvas, generating branded reports, sharing results, giving thumbs up/down feedback, etc.).

The average is calculated by summing Mira usage events over the last 90 days and dividing by the number of active Mira users from the past 12 months. Only existing users are counted; deleted users are not included.

In the session

  • Get them hands-on, screen-share style. This is the best lever. Have the customer share their screen and ask a real question tied to their work, then walk the full loop themselves: review the answer, edit the canvas, generate a branded report, share it, and give feedback. Every step is more usage, and they learn it by doing.
  • Make it a "bring one question" session. If a full workshop is a hard sell, offer 15-20 minutes where they bring one real question they'd normally answer by hand and you work through it together in Mira.
  • Re-run discovery if they went quiet. Ask what they deliver regularly to leadership and what questions colleagues keep asking. If Mira isn't pointed at a recurring need, it won't stick, so find the use case first.
  • Show the share link. For anyone who reports to people outside Meltwater, sharing a response (link, PDF, or email) turns Mira into something they distribute, which gives them a reason to return.

Between calls, when usage slips

  • Send two or three starter questions. Tailor them to their brand, a competitor, or something on their calendar (a launch, earnings, a monthly report), written so they can paste them straight into Mira.
  • Run one for them and send the result. Ask a good question in their Project and email the shareable link with a one-line note ("this took one prompt, here's what came back"). Recipients don't need a Meltwater account to view it.
  • Tie a prompt to their reporting cycle. Send a note before month-end or a board update: "here's a prompt that could pull together your recap."
  • Nudge the lapsed users directly. Check which users have gone quiet and send a short, personal note to them (or via your champion) with one question to try.
  • Point to the mobile app. Mira works there too, so suggest trying one prompt between meetings.
  • Ask your champion what got in the way. A quick "what's stopping you from using Mira?" often surfaces a missing use case or a team that doesn't know it exists.
Draft email: starter questions
Subject: Three questions to try in Mira

Hi [Name],

Here are a few you can paste straight into Mira Studio this week:

1. What were the most impactful stories about [Brand] this week, and what's driving them?
2. How does sentiment toward [Brand] compare to [Competitor] this month?
3. Draft a short executive brief on [upcoming launch or event].

If you share a response with a teammate, there's a link option that doesn't require a Meltwater login. Happy to walk through any of these together if you'd like.

[Your name]

GenAI Lens

One KPI here, and it measures depth rather than breadth: how much each active user is doing in GenAI Lens. When it slips, the account is typically set up and the customer was trained, but nobody is logging back in. The goal is to give them a reason to return and something to do when they get there.

HS20 GenAI Lens 90D

Almost any hands-on activity counts, so the goal is getting users working in the product regularly, not just checking one view. If this is red or yellow, the likely cause is that users aren't returning after training, or they're only looking at results without interacting.

Definition (per internal SOPs)

Average # of GenAI Lens usage events per active user in the last 90 days. Usage is triggered by a wide variety of interactions (e.g. creating prompts, interacting with trends and filters, paginating through results, updating settings, etc).

In the session

  • Get them driving, screen-share style. This is the best lever. Have the customer share their screen and do it themselves: create a new prompt, apply filters, click into Trends, open a competitor in Benchmarking, and drill into a domain in Sources. Doing it live builds the habit and counts as usage.
  • Pick one thing to explore together. Open Benchmarking and ask what they think is driving a competitor gap, or open Sentiment and ask whether a negative theme is accurate. A real question keeps them clicking.
  • Add prompts live. Ask what they or their stakeholders are asking AI tools right now, then have them add those prompts. New prompts create new results to filter and explore.

Between calls, when usage slips

  • Send an a-ha email. Log in, find one or two interesting results (a competitor showing up more than expected, a sentiment shift), and send a short note with a screenshot: "I'm seeing [result] on [topic], worth a look." Specific beats "just checking in."
  • Point them to what's already coming to them. AI Recommendations arrive every Monday and Visibility Rank Change Alerts notify them when their brand or a competitor moves. Make sure the right people know about them and have alerts turned on.
  • Send a report. A branded Web Report or PowerPoint gives them something to forward internally, which pulls other stakeholders in. Each report counts toward their contracted allotment and takes about 30 minutes to generate, so use it when it's worth it.
  • Tie prompts to what's happening now. Ask them to add three prompts about an upcoming launch, campaign, or news event so there's fresh data to check.
  • Bring in a second team. Ask your champion to loop in the SEO / content owner. The Sources tab is the bridge between PR and SEO, and another active user raises the usage average.
  • Check Google Analytics. If GA4 isn't connected, offer to get their GA admin on a quick call. The AI Site Traffic tab ties AI visibility to sessions and revenue, which gives finance-minded stakeholders a reason to log in.
  • Share the GEO Playbook. For content and PR teams who've gone quiet, it's a low-effort way to restart the conversation about what to do with the data.
Draft email: a-ha moment
Subject: Something interesting in your GenAI Lens data

Hi [Name],

I was in your account and noticed [specific result, e.g. Competitor X is showing up in more of the buying-intent prompts than [Brand] is]. I've attached a screenshot.

Worth a few minutes when you have them, and I'm happy to dig in together on a quick call.

[Your name]

Media Relations

These two metrics build on each other in practice, even though they're scored independently. A customer needs a media list before outreach makes sense, so work Media List Updates first and let Journalist Outreach follow naturally once there's a real reason to send something.

Media List Updates (90D)

Measures whether the customer is actively building or maintaining a media list. This is usually the easier of the two Media Relations metrics to move, since it doesn't require an active outreach need to justify the activity.

  • Make it a screen-share moment. Walk the customer through how to build a media list, asking probing discovery questions about the types of journalists they'd want to reach, then have them build it live.
  • Pre-build, then hand off. Create a media list for them ahead of time with a deliberately small number of journalists, then show them how to add more contacts to it themselves.
  • Or do it fully async. Build the list offline and send them details on how to add to it or build their own from scratch.
  • Reframe it for not-ready customers. If they don't have an immediate need, position it as a readiness exercise: "I want to make sure you're comfortable with how this works now, so when you do need it, it's easy." A mock scenario where they share their screen and walk through it is the best way to make this stick.

Journalist Outreach (90D)

Measures whether the customer is actually sending outreach to journalists. This is typically the hardest Media Relations metric to move artificially. It depends on the customer having a real reason to reach out, which you can't always manufacture on a timeline.

  • Don't force it. Sequence it. If there's no immediate outreach need, focus your energy on Media List Updates first. A strong list sets up outreach naturally; pushing outreach before the list exists usually doesn't work.
  • Build readiness, not volume. Even without a live campaign, make sure the customer knows the steps and feels comfortable, so the first real outreach isn't also their first time learning the workflow.

Low-stakes ways to build readiness

  • Draft one sample pitch together as a template they can reuse later, even without a campaign to send it on.
  • Have them identify 3 target journalists from their own media list as a dry run, so the next step (actually sending) feels small.
  • Walk through the send flow on a mock list during a workshop, so the mechanics are already familiar when a real need comes up.

Newsletter

One metric here, Articles Read, but it depends entirely on getting a newsletter set up and actually landing in inboxes. The setup work is the unlock. Once it's flowing regularly, the read-through tends to follow.

Newsletter Articles Read (90D)

Measures whether recipients are actually opening and clicking through newsletter content. This starts with having a newsletter live at all. A customer with no template set up has no chance of generating this metric.

  • Get the basics set up first. Make sure a basic branded newsletter template exists in their account before doing anything else. This is the prerequisite for everything below.

For customers who need convincing

  • Lead with reach. Position it as a way to get the daily digest out to a wider team, automated daily or curated manually if they want control over which articles go out.
  • Lead with branding. Unlike the Daily Digest, the Newsletter can be branded to match their company's visual identity, a meaningful differentiator for comms-focused stakeholders.

A second touchpoint worth building in

  • Set up a weekly newsletter alongside the daily digest. Build this in during account setup, or add it later if Articles Read is showing low adoption. It's a simple roundup of the week's brand, competitor, and industry news, sent to the same recipients as the daily digest. Every additional touch in someone's inbox is another chance for a click, and it gives you a natural reason to bring up Newsletter performance on your next call with the customer.

Klear

Both metrics here come down to the same root cause when they're red: the customer simply isn't logging into Klear. Getting them back into the platform with something compelling to look at solves both at once.

Klear Influencer Profiles Viewed (90D)

Measures whether the customer is actively browsing and viewing influencer profiles. If this is red, it's very likely a login problem rather than a feature problem. Start there before assuming the feature itself isn't landing.

  • Diagnose the login gap first. If this metric is red, the customer almost certainly isn't logging into Klear post-training. Focus on getting them back into the platform before anything else.
  • Send a taste of what's there. Share a few interesting influencers over email along with a quick tip on how to run a search to find similar ones. Give them a reason to log in.
  • Make it a workshop activity. Have the customer log in and share their screen during a workshop call. Have them search for influencers and click into a few profiles to dig deeper.
Draft email: a taste of Klear
Subject: A few creators worth a look for [Brand]

Hi [Name],

I pulled a few influencers that fit [Brand]: [Name 1], [Name 2], and [Name 3]. Here's a quick way to find more like them in Klear: [one-line search tip].

If you'd like, we can do 15 minutes on a screen-share and look through profiles together.

[Your name]

Klear Campaigns Opened (90D)

Measures whether the customer is opening and engaging with campaigns in Klear. A compelling sample campaign, built before they ever log in, gives them something concrete to react to instead of a blank slate.

  • Pre-build a sample campaign. Before the first training, create a sample campaign using influencers they've likely worked with before. Ask the client for a list, or find a few relevant examples directly in Klear.
  • Make the campaign compelling, not generic. A campaign built around influencers they actually recognize lands far better than a generic walkthrough example.
  • Hand over campaign management. Make sure they know how to manage campaigns themselves in Klear. This is another good opportunity to have them share their screen and drive.
